# Literature search — PD and BEF in multitrophic systems (2026-10-15)

Tools: Wiley Scholar Gateway [SG], Undermind [UM], Consensus [CS]. Only
records returned by the tools are listed; quotes come from abstracts (CS) or
full-text PDFs read by Undermind. Items marked "verify" need checking before
citation.

## Key points for framing

- **The hypothesis being tested is explicit in Srivastava et al. (2012):**
  "We argue that PD may be useful in predicting ecosystem functions in a range
  of communities, from single-trophic to complex networks" (abstract, CS).
- The "PD > richness" literature peaked in 2011–2016 and was followed by
  critiques (2016–2018); the current multitrophic BEF agenda (2024–2026)
  focuses on multitrophic richness, energy flux and networks, not PD. Frame
  the paper as a late, explicit test of an influential hypothesis that was
  never tested with more than two manipulated trophic levels.
- **Rarity of >2 manipulated trophic levels:** no review returned a count.
  Lefcheck et al. (2015): of 94 manipulations, 47 of plants, 16 of herbivores
  and 3 of carnivores ("an extremely small sample size"), each manipulating a
  single group. Douglass et al. (2008): "few have been designed to rigorously
  evaluate … the interactive effects of simultaneous diversity change at
  multiple levels". Eisenhauer et al. (2019): "phylogenetic diversity as well
  as consequences for ecosystem functioning are poorly studied" (p. 28).
- Closest cross-trophic PD–EF study: Thompson et al. (2015, pond plankton):
  Chl a best predicted by zooplankton PD, zooplankton biomass by FD; authors
  note the evidence base across trophic levels is "a few other studies".
- No returned study computes PD across a whole multitrophic community
  (plant–animal split) as in this manuscript (search not exhaustive).

## Journal assessment

Related literature concentrates in *Ecology*, then *Functional Ecology*,
*Oikos* and *Freshwater Biology*. Recommended order: **Oikos** (conceptual
reanalyses; O'Connor et al.; Swan & Sparkman), then **Ecology** (Article; the
original experiments and core PD–EF papers; needs strong novelty), then
*Functional Ecology*; *Freshwater Biology* is safe but narrows the general
argument.

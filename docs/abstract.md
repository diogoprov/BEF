**Working title:** Phylogenetic diversity restates trophic structure and does not predict ecosystem functioning in experimental multitrophic food webs

Phylogenetic diversity has been proposed as a better predictor of ecosystem functioning than species richness, including in multitrophic communities, but this idea has rarely been tested in experiments that manipulate diversity across more than two trophic levels. We reanalysed two pond mesocosm experiments in which the richness and composition of macrophytes, grazers and invertebrate predators were manipulated simultaneously (21 and 26 species compositions in 84 and 104 tanks), productivity, respiration and decomposition were measured repeatedly, and half of the tanks of each composition received an acid pulse. We computed metrics representing the richness (PD), divergence (MPD) and regularity (VPD) dimensions of phylogenetic diversity for stocked and realized compositions, and estimated their effects with Bayesian mixed models that accounted for the experimental design, repeated measures and residual autocorrelation. Because every composition combined plants and animals, deep plant–animal divergences dominated phylogenetic distances, and richness and functional-group composition accounted for 89–98% of the among-composition variance in PD and MPD of the stocked communities. At fixed richness and trophic design, no metric credibly affected productivity or respiration, and a weak positive association with decomposition depended on single compositions. Across trophic configurations, explicit functional-group composition explained more of the among-composition variance in productivity and respiration (91% and 80%) than the best single phylogenetic metric (VPD; 70% and 66%), whereas PD explained none. The acid pulse reduced respiration by about one standard deviation, followed by recovery, but phylogenetic metrics did not robustly modulate resistance or recovery. A model of phylogenetically structured species identity effects was not identifiable with these designs. We conclude that, in multitrophic communities, whole-community phylogenetic diversity largely re-describes trophic structure. Testing whether evolutionary history adds predictive power for ecosystem functioning in food webs requires designs in which relatedness varies within trophic levels independently of trophic structure.

**Keywords:** biodiversity–ecosystem functioning, food webs, mesocosm, phylogenetic diversity, trophic structure, disturbance, Bayesian mixed models

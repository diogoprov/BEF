# Title

Whole-community phylogenetic diversity restates trophic structure in experimental pond food webs

Running title: Phylogenetic diversity in experimental food webs

# Abstract

Phylogenetic diversity has been proposed as a summary of community composition that predicts ecosystem functioning from single trophic levels to complex food webs. However, this idea has rarely been tested in experiments that manipulate more than two trophic levels, where relatedness may be confounded with trophic structure. Here, we reanalysed two pond mesocosm experiments in which the richness and composition of macrophytes, grazers and invertebrate predators were manipulated simultaneously (21 and 26 species compositions in 84 and 104 tanks), productivity, respiration and decomposition were measured repeatedly, and half of the tanks received an acid pulse. We computed the phylogenetic diversity, mean pairwise distance and variance of pairwise distances of stocked and realized compositions, and estimated their effects with Bayesian mixed models that accounted for the design and repeated measures. Richness and functional-group composition accounted for 89–98% of the among-composition variance in phylogenetic diversity and mean pairwise distance of the stocked communities, because divergences between plants and animals dominated phylogenetic distances. At a fixed richness and trophic structure, no metric credibly affected any process, and the few effects on recovery after the pulse depended on a single composition or were not replicated between experiments. Across trophic configurations, functional-group composition explained more of the among-composition variance in productivity and respiration (91% and 80%) than the best single metric (70% and 66%), whereas phylogenetic diversity explained almost none. Moreover, a model of phylogenetically structured species effects was not identifiable, and simulations showed that treating compositions that share species as independent inflated false positives up to 52%. Therefore, in multitrophic communities, whole-community phylogenetic diversity seems to re-describe trophic structure rather than add information about it. Testing whether evolutionary history matters for functioning in food webs requires manipulating relatedness within trophic levels, from large species pools, and analyses that model shared species.

Keywords: biodiversity–ecosystem functioning, community assembly, disturbance, mesocosm, multitrophic, species identity
